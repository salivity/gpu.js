Eulerian Smoke and Fire Simulation in GPU.js

Grid-based Eulerian fluid dynamics can be used to simulate smoke and fire in GPU.js, though practical implementations face distinct architectural constraints. By leveraging GPU.js to compile JavaScript functions into WebGL fragment shaders, developers can solve the Navier-Stokes equations across discrete grid cells entirely on the GPU. While 2D simulations are straightforward and performant, true 3D volumetric simulations require custom data-packing strategies and multi-pass rendering pipelines to overcome the absence of native 3D compute shaders in standard WebGL contexts.

The Eulerian Framework for Fire and Smoke

The Eulerian approach treats fluid flow from fixed points in space, dividing the simulation domain into a grid of cells. For smoke and fire, each cell typically stores:

  • Velocity Vector (\(u, v, w\)): The directional movement of fluid throughout the grid.
  • Smoke Density: The concentration of visible particulate matter.
  • Temperature: The thermal energy driving buoyancy forces upward.
  • Fuel/Reaction Coordinates: The unburnt material that combusts into heat and smoke.

The simulation advances each time step through four primary stages governed by the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations: advection (transporting quantities along the velocity field), external forces (buoyancy and vorticity confinement), diffusion (viscous smoothing), and projection (enforcing mass conservation by solving the Poisson pressure equation).

Implementing the Pipeline in GPU.js

GPU.js allows mathematical operations over large datasets to be accelerated by mapping output indices directly to GPU thread IDs. An Eulerian solver in GPU.js relies on chaining multiple computational kernels.

1. Advection

Advection moves fluid properties through the velocity field using semi-Lagrangian backtracing. A GPU.js kernel samples the previous state by tracing backward in time from the current cell along its velocity vector, interpolating the values of surrounding cells.

2. Buoyancy and Combustion

Fire generates upward momentum proportional to its temperature relative to the ambient air. In a GPU.js kernel, external accelerations are applied directly to the vertical velocity component:

\[\mathbf{f}_{\text{buoyancy}} = (-\alpha \rho + \beta (T - T_0))\mathbf{g}\]

where \(\rho\) is smoke density, \(T\) is temperature, \(T_0\) is ambient temperature, and \(\mathbf{g}\) is the gravity vector. Fuel is consumed at a set reaction rate, simultaneously increasing temperature and depositing smoke density.

3. Pressure Projection (The Poisson Solve)

Incompressible fluid requires a divergence-free velocity field (\(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0\)). This step calculates a pressure scalar field to correct velocities:

  1. Compute Divergence: A kernel evaluates the net flow into or out of each cell based on adjacent velocity values.
  2. Pressure Solve: The Poisson equation (\(\nabla^2 p = \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}\)) is solved iteratively using the Jacobi method. In GPU.js, this requires repeatedly ping-ponging two texture buffers across dozens of iterations per frame.
  3. Velocity Correction: A final kernel subtracts the pressure gradient (\(\nabla p\)) from the intermediate velocity field.

2D vs. 3D Volumetric Challenges in GPU.js

2D Simulations

GPU.js handles 2D Eulerian simulations efficiently. Textures natively map to 2D arrays, making neighbor lookups (left, right, up, down) simple index offsets that execute rapidly on standard hardware.

3D Volumetric Simulations

While GPU.js supports 3D output dimensions (kernel.setOutput([width, height, depth])), it internally abstracts 3D arrays over 2D WebGL textures. This abstraction introduces specific engineering hurdles for volumetric smoke and fire:

  • Texture Flattening and Memory Bandwidth: GPU.js emulates 3D memory by tiling or flattening data across 2D render targets. This increases the overhead of trilinear interpolation and spatial neighborhood sampling required by Jacobi iterations and semi-Lagrangian advection.
  • Ping-Pong Overhead: Updating 3D grid states requires multiple independent read/write buffers. Passing large 3D array references between kernels in GPU.js introduces WebGL frame-buffer binding overhead that can degrade real-time framerates at resolutions higher than \(64^3\) or \(128^3\).

Rendering Volumetric Data

Simulating the data grid produces raw values that require volumetric rendering to visualize. In a pure GPU.js workflow, visualization is separated from the solver:

  • Raymarching: A specialized rendering kernel casts rays from the camera through the 3D bounding box of the grid.
  • Absorption and Emission: At each step along a ray, the kernel samples density and temperature. Density attenuates the background light (Beer-Lambert law), while high temperatures sample a blackbody radiation color map to emit the characteristic glow of fire.

Practical Assessment

Eulerian smoke and fire simulations can be computed in GPU.js, serving as an effective educational or prototyping tool for browser-based fluid dynamics. However, for production-grade 3D volumetric fire requiring high grid resolutions (\(256^3\) and above) and complex boundary interactions, raw WebGL2 with 3D textures or modern WebGPU compute shaders provide significantly better memory management, texture sampling control, and execution performance than the high-level abstractions of GPU.js.
